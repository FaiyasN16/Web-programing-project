EDUCATIONAL REFERENCE

Classical Cryptography Guide

Understand the mathematical foundation, history, advantages, and vulnerabilities of classical cipher systems.

~100 BC • Ancient Rome

Caesar Cipher

One of the earliest and simplest encryption techniques. It is a substitution cipher where each letter in the plaintext is shifted by a fixed number of positions down the alphabet.

MATHEMATICAL FORMULA:
E(x) = (x + k) mod 26
D(x) = (x - k) mod 26
Where x is letter index (A=0, Z=25) and k is the shift key (1–25).
Working Example (Shift k = 3):
Plain: H E L L O
Shift: +3 +3 +3 +3 +3
Cipher: K H O O R
✓ Advantages: Extremely fast, easy to calculate by hand, intuitive educational model.
✗ Vulnerability: Trivial to crack using brute-force (only 25 possible keys) or frequency analysis.
Classical Era • Monoalphabetic

Substitution Cipher

Replaces each letter of the plaintext with a predetermined partner from a shuffled 26-letter substitution alphabet. The mapping is one-to-one and reversible.

PERMUTATION KEYSPACE:
Keys = 26! ≈ 4.03 × 10^26
Massive key space makes raw brute-force search computationally infeasible.
Working Example (QWERTY Key):
Plain: A B C D E
Maps: ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓
Cipher: Q W E R T
✓ Advantages: Over 400 septillion possible keys prevents straightforward key trial.
✗ Vulnerability: Preserves single-letter frequencies. In English, 'E', 'T', and 'A' stand out clearly.
1553 • Polyalphabetic

Vigenère Cipher

Dubbed le chiffre indéchiffrable (the indecipherable cipher) for 300 years. It uses a repeating keyword to apply different Caesar shifts to successive letters.

POLYALPHABETIC SHIFT:
C[i] = (P[i] + K[i mod m]) mod 26
P[i] = (C[i] - K[i mod m]) mod 26
Keyword advances only when processing alphabetic characters.
Working Example (Keyword = "LEMON"):
Plain: A T T A C K
Key: L E M O N L
Cipher: L X F O P V
✓ Advantages: Flattens letter frequencies by encoding the same plaintext letter differently.
✗ Vulnerability: Broken by Kasiski examination and Friedman test to find keyword length.

Academic & Educational Advisory: Classical vs Modern Security

Classical ciphers (Caesar, monoalphabetic substitution, Vigenère) are historical algorithms created for human pen-and-paper computation. They do not provide mathematical confidentiality against modern computers and must never be used for sensitive personal, medical, or financial data. Real-world encryption relies on audited modern standards such as AES-GCM (256-bit symmetric) and RSA-4096 / Ed25519 (asymmetric public key).
